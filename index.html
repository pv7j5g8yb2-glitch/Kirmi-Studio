Maison of Aesthetics · Chicago

0:00 / 0:45
What was happening

About 300 people a month were getting in touch. Plenty of interest — the trouble started the moment someone raised their hand.

Roughly 28 calls a month rang out. Messages sat about two hours before anyone replied. Only two thirds of inquiries were followed up at all, and those usually got one or two messages before going cold.

Why it mattered

Every one of those people had already chosen Maison and asked. They were being lost after the hard part was done.

  • How fast you got a reply depended on how busy the desk was that day.
  • Following up relied on someone remembering.
  • Anyone who got in touch after closing waited until morning at best.
  • Nobody could see who had been chased and who hadn't.
What changed

Every message now gets an answer straight away, whatever the hour. Missed calls get a text back within seconds. Anyone who goes quiet hears from us four or five more times instead of once or twice.

When someone's ready to talk properly, the clinic's team picks up the whole conversation — not a name with no context.

What happened after

Replies went from about two hours to six minutes. Missed calls dropped from 28 a month to 10. Inquiries that got followed up rose from 65–70% to about 94%, leaving roughly 15 a month still not worked properly.

Inquiries that became consultations went from about 22% to 29% — around 66 a month to around 87, with no extra inquiries coming in. The team got nine hours a week back.

Levitate Med Spa · New York City

0:00 / 0:39
What was happening

Three locations, about 750 inquiries a month, roughly 360 of them phone calls. The business had got bigger. The way patients were looked after hadn't kept up.

Between 65 and 75 calls a month went unanswered. Messages waited nearly two hours. About 70% got followed up in good time, all of it by hand — one or two attempts, depending on which location and which person picked it up.

Why it mattered

The same patient could be looked after well at one location and badly at another. At three sites, that inconsistency is the business.

  • The service someone got depended on which of the three desks answered.
  • The busiest hours were exactly when staff had least time to reply.
  • Messages piled up overnight and got worked through unevenly.
What changed

One standard across all three, so what a patient gets no longer depends on where their message lands. Every inquiry gets an immediate reply, whatever the location or hour. Missed calls get contacted automatically.

Each location keeps its own calendar, its own team, and its own handover point.

What happened after

Replies went from about an hour forty-five to eight minutes. Missed calls fell from 65–75 a month to around 30, with about 86% contacted automatically. Follow-up rose from around 70% to 95%. More than 90% of people who message after closing now hear back right away.

Inquiries that became consultations went from about 20% to 27% — around 150 a month to around 203, with no extra inquiries coming in. Chasing dropped from 30 hours a week to 13 across the group.

Soho Skin Lab · Miami

0:00 / 0:41
What was happening

Soho Skin Lab had about 4,800 patients on file who had gone quiet — people who had booked, paid and been treated before, and simply stopped coming back.

This one is different from the other two. New inquiries were being handled fine. The money sitting untouched was behind the clinic, not in front of it.

Why it mattered

The clinic had already paid to win every one of these patients once. Getting them back costs nothing like winning a new one.

  • They were scattered across different systems, with no agreed idea of what "stopped coming" even meant.
  • Reaching out had only happened now and then, by hand, with no record of it.
  • Even when an old patient showed interest, nothing carried them through to a booking.
What changed

We got back in touch over 90 days — spread out over time and written around what each person had actually had done, rather than one mass message to everybody.

Anyone who replied with real interest went straight into the same booking process we run for new inquiries, so a tempted old patient reaches a consultation instead of a dead end.

What happened after

We got a message to about 3,400 of them. Around 260 wrote back genuinely interested. That became more than 70 consultations, more than 40 patients coming back, and more than 30 going ahead with treatment.

That came to over $125,000 in treatment revenue from patients who had stopped coming. To be clear about that number: it's treatment actually delivered and paid for in those 90 days. It isn't a forecast, and it doesn't mean all 4,800 were ever going to buy.
